## Summary

Behavioral rounds in the notes are STAR prompts: situation, task, action, result. Amazon's July 2024 notes list leadership-principle questions. Meta's notes have an ownership round. Uber's notes include disagreement and learning quickly. This page is the method only.

## Key ideas

- **Situation.** One sentence of context. No employer name required for practice.
- **Task.** What you were responsible for. Not what the team vaguely hoped.
- **Action.** What you did, in order. This is most of the answer. Use I, not we, for the action.
- **Result.** What changed, in a way you could measure. Leave the real numbers in your private notes.
- **Length.** About eight to twelve sentences when spoken. Practice out loud.
- **Map.** One story can serve more than one prompt if the action truly fits. Do not force it.
- **Write locally.** Use a private note on your own machine. Do not paste stories into the repo, a ticket, or this browser's synced profile if that profile is shared.

## Interview angles

- The prompt list is on the next page.
- The mock rubric grades whether the answer has an action and a result, not whether it matches a sample.

## Pitfalls

- A situation that takes the whole answer.
- A result with no action.
- A story you invented with fake precision. Use a real one, stored privately, or say you will write it locally.

## Say this in the interview

I keep a private list of prompts and, next to each, the story id I would use. In the room I spend one sentence on the situation, most of the time on what I did, and I end with what changed.
